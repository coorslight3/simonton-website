1031 Exchanges

Defer capital gains by exchanging into like-kind investment property. We help multifamily owners plan the sale and find the right replacement.

A 1031 exchange lets you sell an investment property and reinvest the proceeds in another one without paying capital gains tax at the time of the sale. For owners who have held an apartment building for years, it is often what makes selling worthwhile. Why Owners Exchange

Keep Your Full Equity Working

Deferring the tax means the whole sale proceeds go into the next property instead of a share going to the IRS.

Upgrade or Diversify

Move from an older, management-heavy building into a newer asset, a different market or a more passive property type.

Grow Over Time

Exchanging again and again lets untaxed gains compound into larger properties over the years.

The Rules That Matter

An exchange runs on fixed deadlines that start the day your sale closes. Missing one generally means the sale is taxed, so the replacement search should start before you go into escrow, not after.

45 days to identify
Name your potential replacement properties in writing within 45 days of the sale. Most investors use the three-property rule: up to three properties of any value.
180 days to close
Close on at least one identified property within 180 days of the sale, or by your tax return due date if that comes first.
A qualified intermediary holds the money
Sale proceeds go to a qualified intermediary, not to you. Taking the cash ends the exchange.
Reinvest it all to defer it all
Any proceeds you do not reinvest, and any drop in debt you do not replace, can be taxed. To defer fully, buy property of equal or greater value.
Leverage can help you scale
You can add a new loan on top of your proceeds to buy a larger replacement than your equity alone would allow.

Exchanging Into Net Lease

Many apartment owners use an exchange to step away from day-to-day management. A popular route is net lease property: commercial buildings where the tenant pays rent plus some or all of the property's taxes, insurance and maintenance.

The more expenses the tenant covers, the more passive the investment, and the more the property's value depends on the strength of that one tenant.

Advantages

  • Predictable, long-term rent
  • Low landlord expenses
  • Often leased to national brands
  • Hands-off ownership

Trade-offs

  • Modest rent growth
  • Value tied to one tenant
  • Lease terms are hard to change

What Goes Into a Buy Box

Property Type

Apartments, retail, industrial, office or net lease, chosen for demand, appreciation potential and how hands-on you want to be.

Price & Leverage

A target purchase price and a loan-to-value you are comfortable with, sized to reinvest your full proceeds.

Cap Rate

The return you expect, weighed against risk, location and the stability of the income.

Lease Terms

How long the lease runs (often 10 years or more for security) and which structure: NNN, NN or gross.

Location

Target markets near growth and demand, checked against local rents and, for retail, traffic counts.

Tenant

Industry, track record and credit. Investment-grade tenants reduce risk; local operators can offer higher yield with more risk.

Demographics

Population and household income that support the tenant's business or the property's renters.

Building

Size, single or multi-tenant, condition and rent per square foot compared with the market.

What Drives a Replacement Property's Value

Location & Building

  • Strong demographics, traffic and visibility
  • Newer or renovated buildings on good sites
  • Fewer capital needs mean a stronger return

Tenant Strength

  • Corporate-guaranteed tenants, often rated by S&P or Moody's, are the most secure
  • Franchisee guarantees depend on the operator's size and finances
  • Local businesses can pay more but carry more risk

Lease & Pricing

  • Longer remaining terms add security
  • Built-in rent increases help offset inflation
  • Renewal options extend the income
  • A cap rate below comparable sales signals a premium asset
